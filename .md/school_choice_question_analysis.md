## Main Themes From These Questions

- Parents want first-hand experience, not brochure language. They repeatedly ask for honest pros, cons, culture, discipline, and real outcomes.

- Comparison questions are central. The strongest signals come from posts that ask families to choose between two named schools or between two routes, such as grammar versus Catholic, state versus private, or current school versus transfer.

- Fit matters as much as prestige. Parents care about sensitivity, pressure level, co-ed versus single-sex, religion, commute, support, and whether a child will genuinely thrive.

- Backup planning is a major concern. Many questions are really about fallback routes: reserve lists, Plan B grammars, backup sixth forms, feeder schools, or what to do after a failed 11+ or 13+ outcome.

- Money is often present even when parents do not lead with it. Scholarships, fees, value for money, and the trade-off between strong state schools and expensive private schools appear repeatedly.

- Admissions rules create uncertainty. Families worry about hidden expectations, unclear in-year admission tactics, reserve-list odds, and whether stated criteria match real practice.

- The most useful "school-choice" questions are the ones that help a parent reduce uncertainty before committing to a school, a catchment, or an admissions pathway.
